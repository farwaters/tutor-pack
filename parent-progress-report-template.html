DRAFT - Parent Progress Report Template (longer-period summary)

STATUS: DRAFT for adult review only. Not sent. The safeguarding lead translates this and sends it personally. CEFR range: Pre-A1 to A2+ | Age band: 6-12 | Length: about one page

What this is: the longer-period summary (for example every 6-8 weeks or one term). A short note after each lesson is a separate template: see parent-session-note-template.md. The level indicator appears here (and in the level check), not in the per-session note. Terms (Tutor, human tutor, safeguarding lead) are defined in parent-in-the-loop-design.md, section 1.

Rules for this report: first name and age band only. No exact birth date, surname, school, address or photo. No medical, legal or counselling statements. Do not compare the child with other children. No mood or personality words about the child (no "shy", "happy and kind" and so on); write what the child did. No referral wording: any suggestion to see a teacher or specialist is the safeguarding lead's own decision, made by hand. If an item has been hard for several sessions, use only: "This has been hard for several sessions. We will try a different way to practise it. The safeguarding lead is happy to talk it through with you."

TEMPLATE (copy and fill in)

Progress report

Child: [first name], age band [6-7 / 7-8 / 9-10 / 10-12] Period covered: [date] to [date] ([number] lessons) Prepared by: Tutor (draft). Checked and sent by the safeguarding lead.

1. Level now [First name] is working at [Pre-A1 / A1 / A2 / A2+]. (This is a guide, not an official exam result.)

What [first name] can do now: - [Can-do statement 1, for example: "I can say and understand 20 animal words."] - [Can-do statement 2] - [Can-do statement 3]

2. Strengths [1-2 short sentences. Be specific.]

3. Next steps [1-2 short sentences about what we will practise next.]

4. How you can help at home (5 minutes is enough) - [Idea 1] - [Idea 2] - [Idea 3, optional]

5. Attendance and engagement [One line: "[First name] came to [x] of [y] lessons and joined in [very well / well / with some help]."]

6. Closing [One or two warm sentences. Thank the child and the parent.]

EXAMPLE (INVENTED DATA - NOT A REAL CHILD)

"Example child: Child A, age band 7-8." Child A is invented. All details below are made up to show how to fill in the template.

Progress report

Child: Child A, age band 7-8 Period covered: 7 September to 30 October (8 lessons) Prepared by: Tutor (draft). Checked and sent by the safeguarding lead.

1. Level now Child A is working at A1. (This is a guide, not an official exam result.)

What Child A can do now: - I can say the time on the hour, for example "It's seven o'clock." - I can say six daily actions, for example "I get up" and "I eat breakfast." - I can ask and answer "What time do you get up?" with a picture to help.

2. Strengths Child A listened carefully and answered quickly. Child A joined in with every game and tried new sentences. Child A's "three" and "eat" were clear.

3. Next steps We will practise "half past" and short sentences about weekends. We will also practise the small word "at" (for example "at seven o'clock").

4. How you can help at home - Ask Child A one question in English each day, for example "What time do you eat dinner?" Accept a short answer. - Let Child A teach you three new English words each week. - Watch one short, child-friendly English video together and talk about what you liked.

5. Attendance and engagement Child A came to 8 of 8 lessons and joined in very well.

6. Closing Child A joined in with every game. Thank you for your support. We look forward to the next lessons.

TRANSLATION NOTES (for the safeguarding lead)

  • Keep sentences short. One idea per sentence.
  • Avoid idioms (for example "on track", "good with words", "keen as mustard"). Use plain words.
  • Keep the level names (Pre-A1, A1, A2, A2+) in English. Add a short local explanation if needed.
  • Keep the example English words and sentences (for example "It's seven o'clock") in English inside the translated text, so parents know what the child says.
  • Times and dates: use the local format (for example the day-and-month order used locally).
  • Check names: keep the child's first name exactly as the family uses it. Do not add a surname.
  • Tone: warm, honest and positive. Do not promise exam results.
  • Do not include: medical, legal or counselling words; comparisons with other children; any personal data beyond the first name and age band.
  • If a sentence feels hard to translate, shorten it. Ask Tutor to reword it before sending.
